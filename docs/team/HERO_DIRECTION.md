# Hero direction: "The fog is thinking" (Steve, 5 Nov 2026). One direction. Binding for art and frontend.

Four rounds gave us places: stills, a particle skyline, a wireframe city, a silver model of the Bay. Each one was a map, and none had a person in it. That's why it reads as random. The map goes. A person comes in.

## 1. The idea
In the first three seconds you see a young person sitting alone on a dark San Francisco hill at night. The fog is pouring over the ridge across the city and glowing from inside, and a slow pulse of light comes through it and reaches them.
SF's fog has always rolled in at night. Now the whole city is lit up and thinking, and some of that light is coming for one person. That's Tal. It's an A24 frame, not a stock photo: one human, still, small, under something enormous and alive.

## 2. The still
- **Subject:** a hill above the city. On its crest, small and left of centre, one person sits on the grass with their knees up, seen from behind. A closed laptop lies beside them. Below them, the city is a carpet of soft lights on steep streets of small houses. Beyond the city, fog pours over the long western ridge, lit from inside. Up close, its leading edge breaks into very fine filaments, like mycelium or neurons, that you only see on a second look. That's the trippy part. A three-pronged broadcast tower rises out of the fog as a silhouette. It's the only landmark.
- **Camera and light:** 35 mm film, 40 mm lens, low on the grass, level horizon. The only light is the city and the fog. The figure is a silhouette with a faint rim of fog light.
- **Palette:** black, silver and white only, true R=G=B after the grade. **No accent:** the light itself is the idea.
- **Layout:** the top 35 % is pure black sky (mean ≤ 8/255) for the wordmark. The fog's glow sits just under the ridge at centre and backlights "tal". The lower-centre third is dark grass (mean ≤ 20/255), where the sub, pill and fine print sit. The figure sits clear of the copy column: on 16:9 at x ≈ 20 %, head ≈ 56 %, height ≈ 10 % of the frame; on 4:5 at x ≈ 24 %, head ≈ 52 %, with the crest at ≈ 62 %.
- **Model:** `ART_MODEL=gemini-3-pro-image`. Add `imageConfig.imageSize: "4K"` in `scripts/art/gen.mjs`, since it sends no size today. Generate eight 16:9 takes. Make the 4:5 from the chosen 16:9 as a reference image ("Recompose this exact scene, same person, same fog, same tower, for a vertical 4:5 phone frame: ridge at 40 %, crest at 62 %, person at 24 % from the left, top 35 % pure black"). Then run the round-5 grade: neutralise to R=G=B and apply the top fade.

## 3. The motion: one plate, one WebGL shader. No video, no parallax layers, no crossfades.
The still is the LCP `<picture>`. A WebGL canvas fades in over it after `load` and idle, with the same object-cover maths. It does three things and nothing else:
1. **The fog breathes.** A domain-warped noise offset moves the plate's UVs inside a feathered fog mask. Build the mask (`public/art/hero-fog-mask.png`, 1024 px, grey) from a luminance key in the fog band, then clean it by hand. It drifts downhill toward the city at ≤ 8 px/s at 1440, with a maximum offset of 0.35 % of the width. Time runs on a circle in noise space, so the loop is seamless at **30 s**.
2. **Light finds the person.** There are three authored paths through the fog (`hero-paths.json`, normalised coordinates for both crops), each running from the far ridge to the figure's crest. A pulse is a soft gaussian lift along about 8 % of the path's length, peaking at +18 % luminance. It travels for 4.5 s with ease-in-out. On arrival, the grass around the figure lifts +6 % for 600 ms, like a breath. There's one pulse every 10 s and never two at once, so the three paths make the 30 s loop. **The first pulse starts 0.6 s after the wordmark lands and arrives by 3.0 s.** That's the dopamine.
3. **Film.** Luminance-only grain at 1 device px, amplitude 0.035, refreshed at 24 fps (it replaces the CSS `.grain` while WebGL runs), plus a gentle bloom on values above 200/255.

It must never: move the person; draw particles, dots, nodes or connecting lines; flash; show colour; show logos (delete the landmark marks and hub pulses from the hero; the marquee keeps the logos); put text in the image; recreate anything from HBO's Silicon Valley; follow the cursor; or move faster than real fog. `prefers-reduced-motion`, Save-Data, no WebGL or a dropped context: the still alone, which is complete on its own. Budget: ≤ 4 ms per frame on a 2020 MacBook Air; on phones, DPR ≤ 2 and 30 fps.

## 4. Quality bar
- Generate at 4K and never upscale. Desktop 16:9 widths: 1440 / 2160 / **2880** / 3840 (AVIF q≈62 plus WebP). Phone 4:5: 828 / 1170 / **1600**. The WebGL texture is 2880 wide on desktop and 1600 on phones. Today's 1376 px hero is soft on a 1440 retina, and that's a fail.
- Grain is fine in the plate and alive in the shader, never blotchy. The fog's peak stays ≤ 230/255, so the white pill stays the brightest object on the page.
- The sub (white/72, or up to /80 if it must) sits on dark grass at ≥ 4.5:1 contrast, measured on the actual pixels. Keep one soft radial scrim (alpha ≤ 0.5) at most. Remove the right-edge vignette, since the Pacific plane is gone. Keep the wordmark, the one line and the one white pill exactly as they are.

## 5. Acceptance test (self-check before you call me)
1. Show five people the still with no text: they say "San Francisco" within three seconds, and none of them says "stock" or "AI".
2. Measure: top 35 % mean ≤ 8/255, copy zone ≤ 20/255, sub contrast ≥ 4.5:1, fog peak ≤ 230, R=G=B, served widths ≥ 2880 (desktop) and ≥ 1600 (phone), clean at 200 % zoom on a 1440 retina.
3. Motion: the first pulse reaches the figure by 3.0 s, pulses never overlap, the fog drifts ≤ 8 px/s, the person never moves, the loop is 30 s and seamless, reduced motion shows the still, frames take ≤ 4 ms, and the LCP is unchanged.
4. The frame has no text, logo, bridge, named tower, face, glowing screen or particle, and the landmark logos are gone from the hero.
5. An AI engineer at Anthropic or OpenAI in SF sees it and says "wow, I should try this."
